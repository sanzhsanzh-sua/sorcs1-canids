SorCS1 across canids

Group-level amino-acid variation in the SorCS1 receptor across fennec fox, Belyaev farm foxes, and domestic dog. Over the ~90% of the coding sequence that is callable, the protein is near-invariant — with a single confirmed Canis/Vulpes substitution that lands on a predicted ligand-contact residue of the VPS10p β-propeller.

The receptor, and what could be read

SorCS1 (VPS10-domain receptor, gene SORCS1) is a type-I single-pass neuronal sorting receptor: a large ten-bladed β-propeller (the VPS10p / Vps10p domain) followed by two cysteine-rich 10CC modules, a PKD domain, a single transmembrane helix, and a short cytoplasmic tail.

Fennec and dog variants are called against the dog assembly CanFam3.1, in which the first 190 residues of SorCS1 are absent (signal peptide, propeptide, and the first propeller blades — confirmed missing by both NCBI Gnomon and a fresh miniprot alignment; not an assembly gap). The Belyaev foxes sit on the complete VulVul3 assembly. The analysis window on the CanFam3.1 side is therefore residues 191–1202 in dog isoform-X5 numbering (≈ residues 187–1168 of human canonical Q8WY21).

The upper mark is the substitution seen between the genera — S568N in dog isoform-X5 numbering, residue 564 here — sitting 5 residues before Asp-box repeat 4 (569–580). The lower mark is the domestic-dog polymorphism, dog S625N = human residue 621.

The one difference: S568N

One codon in the whole readable region changes the protein between fennec and dog. In dog isoform-X5 numbering it is residue 568 (human canonical 564, red-fox X2 574). The reference dog serine is the Canis state; both Vulpes species carry asparagine, and human carries threonine — so this is a genus-level Canis/Vulpes difference, not something unique to the fennec.

Structural placement — by homology to SorCS2–NGF

No experimental structure of any SorCS β-propeller exists. The closest is the mouse SorCS2 ectodomain bound to NGF (PDB 6FFY, 3.9 Å; SorCS1–SorCS2 45.9% identity). Aligning the two receptors, SorCS1 residue 564 corresponds to SorCS2 residue 540 —

Finding

SorCS2 residue 540 is 3.3 Å from NGF and is one of the 21 residues in the direct ligand-binding footprint. The single confirmed Canis/Vulpes coding difference in SorCS1 therefore falls on a predicted ligand-contact position of the β-propeller.

The substitution itself is chemically conservative — S, T and N are all small polar side chains that can hydrogen-bond — so this is a testable hypothesis about a shift in propeller ligand affinity or specificity between the two genera, not a demonstrated functional change. The homology mapping, the low structural resolution, and the partly non-overlapping ligand repertoires of SorCS1 and SorCS2 are all caveats.

What isn’t there

The rest of the readout is negative, and that is itself the result:

Group-consensus proteins are 99.9–100% identical to their species references over all callable positions. The ~27 fixed fennec–dog differences are concentrated at synonymous sites — the signature of strong purifying selection on the SorCS1 protein. Between the tame and aggressive fox lines there is no coding difference in SORCS1; with n = 5 + 4 at ~3× and only ~55% of the CDS callable per sub-group, this is “no evidence of a difference,” not proof of its absence.

Read these results within

The N-terminal 190 residues (signal peptide + first propeller blades) are absent from CanFam3.1 and were not assessed for fennec or dog. ~10% (fennec) to ~13% (pooled fox) of the CDS is uncallable at group level; per fox sub-group it is ~45%. Fennec and Belyaev-fox genomes are ~3× low-pass.

Pipeline

  1. Assembly ID. Belyaev fox VCFs traced to GCF_048418805.1 / VulVul3; chromosomes 3 & 15 fetched, gene model extracted.
  2. Reference models. miniprot rebuilt a complete CanFam3.1 SORCS1 CDS (26 exons, residues 191–1202, 100% identity to dog RefSeq isoform X5); fox model = XM_025990732.2. Exon-for-exon correspondence confirmed to the nucleotide.
  3. Indexing. bgzip + tabix on all three VCFs; bcftools norm left-aligned indels, 0 REF mismatches.
  4. Dog group. Remote tabix slice of the NHGRI 722-canid VCF (Plassais et al., 2019; 332 GB on server) — SORCS1 region pulled in ~70 s without downloading the file. 20 samples: 10 village dogs + 10 wolves.
  5. Consensus. Per-sample CDS built (exon-wise, both alleles), then set aside: at DP ≥ 3, 25–95% of each fennec CDS and 28–62% of each fox CDS masks to N. Analysis moved to group level.
  6. Group catalogue. Per codon, per group: major allele among samples callable at DP ≥ 3, minimum 3 samples; multi-SNV codons combined; high / low confidence by the 50% threshold.
  7. Alignment & table. MAFFT L-INS-i of 5 group consensuses + dog / fox / human references; variant-codon tables in both coordinate systems.
  8. Structure. AF-Q8WY21-F1 for domain placement and pLDDT; SorCS2–NGF (PDB 6FFY) for the ligand-interface test.
